# Epistemic Discipline Improvements for Deep Research Prompts

## Summary

Based on analysis of the MRI Software LLC Strategic Overview report, we've identified 7 key issues with epistemic discipline in long documents generated by the Accordion Method. This document outlines proposed improvements to the Deep Research prompts.

## Issues Identified

### 1. Hypothesis Drift Across Sections
**Problem**: Sections lose confidence calibration as the document progresses. Early sections use appropriate hedging ("This suggests..."), but later sections state the same ideas as confirmed facts.

**Example from MRI Report**:
- Section 2: "There may be a significant opportunity to help MRI harmonize these acquisitions (Hypothesis)"
- Section 15: "MRI's rapid growth has created integration debt that is limiting R&D velocity"

**Proposed Fix**: Add section-level confidence reset instructions to the section writing prompt.

```yaml
section_confidence_reset: |
  CONFIDENCE CALIBRATION: Each section starts fresh. Do NOT inherit confidence 
  from previous sections. If a hypothesis was stated earlier, it remains a 
  hypothesis here unless you have NEW evidence to upgrade it.
  
  Before writing, review your confidence labels:
  - Confirmed: Only if you have direct evidence in THIS section
  - Reported: Only if citing a specific source
  - Estimated: Only if showing calculation
  - Hypothesis: Default for interpretations and inferences
```

### 2. Conflating Incentives with Intent
**Problem**: Assuming companies intend what their incentives suggest, without evidence of actual intent.

**Example from MRI Report**:
- "Given MRI's current strategic flux and its stated goal of a $10 billion exit..."
- "MRI appears to be optimizing its operations for a $10 billion valuation"

**Proposed Fix**: Add intent vs. incentive disambiguation guidance.

```yaml
intent_vs_incentive: |
  INTENT VS. INCENTIVE: Distinguish what incentives suggest from what the 
  company has stated they intend.
  
  WRONG: "They are optimizing for an IPO" (assumes intent)
  RIGHT: "PE ownership creates incentives to optimize for exit, though the 
  company has not publicly stated IPO plans (Hypothesis)"
  
  WRONG: "They want to become the platform of record"
  RIGHT: "Their 'Open and Connected' positioning suggests a platform strategy, 
  though we'd need to validate their actual strategic priorities (Hypothesis)"
```

### 3. Lack of Falsification Checks
**Problem**: No systematic challenge of major hypotheses. Once a hypothesis is stated, it's developed but never questioned.

**Example from MRI Report**:
- Central hypothesis: "MRI's long-term value lies in its transition from software vendor to data orchestrator"
- Never challenged with: "What if they're actually just a financial system of record with good integrations?"

**Proposed Fix**: Add falsification check instructions for major hypotheses.

```yaml
falsification_checks: |
  FALSIFICATION: For each major hypothesis, explicitly consider the counter-case.
  
  After stating a hypothesis, add a "Counter-Hypothesis" subsection:
  - What evidence would disprove this?
  - What alternative explanation fits the same facts?
  - What would we expect to see if this hypothesis were wrong?
  
  Example:
  **Hypothesis**: MRI is transitioning to a data orchestration platform
  **Counter-Hypothesis**: MRI is primarily a financial system of record with 
  good integrations, and "data orchestration" is marketing language rather 
  than strategic reality. Evidence to validate: actual data flows, API usage 
  patterns, customer use cases beyond financial reporting.
```

### 4. Framework Over-Amplification
**Problem**: SWOT, Porter's Five Forces, and Value Chain sections generate new insights instead of organizing existing ones. This creates repetition and confidence drift.

**Example from MRI Report**:
- SWOT introduces "integration debt" as a weakness
- Strategic Tensions restates it as a tension
- Fragilities mentions it again as a systemic risk
- Patterns identifies it as a pattern

**Proposed Fix**: Add framework constraint instructions.

```yaml
framework_constraints: |
  FRAMEWORK SECTIONS (SWOT, Porter's, Value Chain): These are ORGANIZING 
  frameworks, not GENERATIVE sections. Your job is to CATEGORIZE insights 
  already developed in earlier sections, not introduce new ones.
  
  SWOT Analysis:
  - Review sections 1-9 (through Competitive Landscape)
  - Organize existing insights into Strengths/Weaknesses/Opportunities/Threats
  - Do NOT introduce new observations here
  - Each item should reference the section where it was first developed
  
  Porter's Five Forces:
  - Review Industry Dynamics and Competitive Landscape sections
  - Organize existing competitive insights into the five forces framework
  - Do NOT introduce new competitive analysis here
  
  Value Chain Analysis:
  - Review Products/Services and Business Model sections
  - Organize existing value creation insights into value chain framework
  - Do NOT introduce new business model observations here
```

### 5. Audience Confusion
**Problem**: Sections are written for different audiences (board vs analysts vs implementers), creating tonal inconsistency.

**Example from MRI Report**:
- Executive Summary: Written for partners (strategic, high-level)
- Products and Services: Written for analysts (detailed, technical)
- Where They'll Say Yes: Written for sales (tactical, budget-focused)

**Proposed Fix**: Add consistent audience framing.

```yaml
audience_consistency: |
  AUDIENCE: You are writing for a consulting partner preparing for a discovery 
  conversation. Every section should serve that purpose.
  
  NOT for:
  - The client (this is internal prep)
  - Junior analysts (assume strategic sophistication)
  - Sales teams (this is sensemaking, not pitching)
  
  Tone throughout:
  - Analytical and curious
  - Hypothesis-driven
  - Evidence-based
  - Comfortable with uncertainty
  
  Ask yourself: "Would a partner read this section the night before a meeting 
  and feel more prepared?" If not, revise.
```

### 6. Narrative Escalation
**Problem**: Confidence and stakes escalate throughout the document. Early sections are measured, later sections become dramatic.

**Example from MRI Report**:
- Section 1: "MRI stands at a critical strategic juncture" (measured)
- Section 12: "The central hypothesis we wish to validate with MRI is that the company's long-term value lies in its transition..." (elevated)
- Section 18: "If MRI can successfully prove that its 'open' model generates more actionable insights... it will not only justify its $10 billion valuation but potentially set a new standard for how the global real estate industry operates" (dramatic)

**Proposed Fix**: Add narrative ceiling instructions.

```yaml
narrative_ceiling: |
  NARRATIVE CEILING: Maintain consistent stakes throughout. Do NOT escalate 
  drama or confidence as the document progresses.
  
  Avoid escalation patterns:
  - "Critical juncture" → "Existential moment" → "Industry-defining inflection point"
  - "Opportunity" → "Significant opportunity" → "Transformational opportunity"
  - "Hypothesis" → "Strong hypothesis" → "Central thesis"
  
  Maintain consistent framing:
  - Stakes: "Meaningful impact" not "Industry transformation"
  - Confidence: "Worth exploring" not "We believe"
  - Scope: "For this company" not "For the entire industry"
  
  If you find yourself writing "transformational", "revolutionary", "paradigm shift", 
  or "industry-defining", stop and rewrite with more measured language.
```

### 7. Unrealistic "Where They'll Say Yes" Sections
**Problem**: Overly optimistic engagement scenarios that don't account for budget reality, decision-making complexity, or competitive alternatives.

**Example from MRI Report**:
- "Integration Efficiency and Tech Debt Rationalization" - assumes they'll pay consultants to fix acquisition integration
- "AI Monetization and Go-to-Market Strategy" - assumes they need help pricing AI features
- "Capitalizing on Regulatory Shifts" - assumes they'll pay for a "migration and de-risking GTM strategy"

**Proposed Fix**: Add budget reality check instructions.

```yaml
engagement_realism: |
  WHERE THEY'LL SAY YES: Ground recommendations in budget reality and decision-making complexity.
  
  For each opportunity, explicitly address:
  
  1. BUDGET REALITY
     - Is there existing budget for this? (Yes/No/Unclear)
     - If no, what would need to be true to create budget?
     - What's the likely budget range? ($50K? $500K? $5M?)
  
  2. DECISION COMPLEXITY
     - Who owns this decision? (Title, not just "leadership")
     - How many stakeholders need to align?
     - What's the typical decision timeline? (Weeks? Quarters? Years?)
  
  3. COMPETITIVE ALTERNATIVES
     - Can they do this internally?
     - Are there cheaper alternatives? (Software? Contractors? Other consultants?)
     - Why would they choose us over alternatives?
  
  4. PRIORITY SIGNALS
     - Is there evidence they're already working on this?
     - Is this a stated priority or our inference?
     - What would make this urgent vs. "nice to have"?
  
  Be honest about "Budget-Resistant Areas" - where we'd be creating demand, not 
  meeting existing demand. These aren't bad opportunities, but they require 
  different sales approaches and longer timelines.
```

## Implementation Plan

### Phase 1: Update Shared Epistemic Rules (Immediate)
Add the new rules to `src/primr/prompts/shared/epistemic_rules.yaml`:
- `section_confidence_reset`
- `intent_vs_incentive`
- `falsification_checks`
- `framework_constraints`
- `audience_consistency`
- `narrative_ceiling`
- `engagement_realism`

### Phase 2: Update Section Writing Prompt (Immediate)
Modify the `section_writing_prompt` in `src/primr/prompts/company_overview.yaml` to include:
- Confidence reset reminder at the start of each section
- Framework constraint checks for SWOT, Porter's, and Value Chain sections
- Audience consistency reminder
- Narrative ceiling check

### Phase 3: Update Specific Section Instructions (Next Sprint)
For sections prone to issues:
- **SWOT Analysis**: Add explicit "organize, don't generate" instruction
- **Strategic Tensions**: Add "build on SWOT, don't repeat" instruction
- **Where They'll Say Yes**: Add budget reality check template
- **Strategic Positioning Hypothesis**: Add falsification check requirement

### Phase 4: Post-Generation Validation (Future)
Integrate the report analyzer to check for:
- Confidence drift (count hypothesis labels by section)
- Repetition (detect similar phrases across sections)
- Escalation (detect dramatic language in later sections)
- Citation coverage (ensure all claims are supported)

## Testing Plan

1. **Baseline**: Re-run MRI Software LLC research with current prompts
2. **Phase 1**: Re-run with updated epistemic rules
3. **Phase 2**: Re-run with updated section writing prompt
4. **Compare**: Use report analyzer to measure improvements in:
   - Confidence calibration consistency
   - Hypothesis vs. fact ratio
   - Repetition across sections
   - Citation coverage

## Expected Outcomes

- **Confidence Calibration**: Consistent use of (Hypothesis), (Estimated), (Reported), (Confirmed) throughout
- **No Repetition**: Each insight lives in ONE section, cross-referenced elsewhere
- **Falsification**: Major hypotheses include counter-cases
- **Framework Discipline**: SWOT/Porter's/Value Chain organize existing insights, don't generate new ones
- **Audience Consistency**: Every section serves the "partner preparing for discovery" audience
- **Narrative Ceiling**: Stakes and confidence remain consistent throughout
- **Engagement Realism**: "Where They'll Say Yes" grounded in budget reality and decision complexity

## Success Metrics

- Citation coverage: 100% (all claims supported)
- Confidence labels: 50+ per report (showing appropriate hedging)
- Hypothesis ratio: 30-40% of confidence labels should be (Hypothesis)
- Repetition: <5% of sentences appear in multiple sections
- Framework discipline: SWOT/Porter's/Value Chain sections cite earlier sections, don't introduce new insights
- Engagement realism: "Where They'll Say Yes" includes budget reality checks for each opportunity
