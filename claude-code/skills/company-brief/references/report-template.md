# Company brief template (lite primr method)

Target length: 2,000-5,000 words depending on signal. Every section that makes a non-obvious claim labels it and cites it. Keep references numbered inline in the house format `[cite: N]` with one consolidated `## Sources` list at the end (`[cite: N] Title — URL`); do not scatter full URLs through the body.

---

## 1. Executive Summary

Open with a two-to-three sentence position statement: what the company is, who it serves, how it makes money, and the one-line competitive frame. Follow with **Key insights**: 3-5 bullets, each a finding (not a fact restatement), each labeled and cited. The reader should be able to stop here and still be smarter.

## 2. Company Overview

Founding, ownership (public/PE/VC/founder-held), scale indicators (revenue if public or `(Estimated)` from credible reporting, headcount, locations), and trajectory in one or two paragraphs.

## 3. Products and Services

What they actually sell, structured by line. Distinguish flagship from periphery. Note the pricing model if visible (per-seat, consumption, project) and say so if no pricing is published (sales-led signal).

## 4. Target Customers and Go-to-Market

Who buys, in what verticals, at what size. Named customers from case studies. Sales motion as evidenced (self-serve signup vs demo-request, partner channel mentions, marketplace listings).

## 5. Market and Competition

The competitive frame: 3-6 named alternatives and how this company positions against them. Use third-party comparison sources where possible; the company's own "why us" page is `(Reported)` at best about competitors.

## 6. Technology Stack Signals

Triangulate three evidence streams and say which says what:
- DNS recon: email/productivity platform, SaaS verification trail, hosting/CDN hints
- Job postings: named tools and platforms engineers are hired to use
- Site claims: integrations pages, security/compliance pages, engineering blog

Distinguish IT estate (what they run internally) from product infrastructure (what the product is built on). Calibrate per the recon cheatsheet.

## 7. Hiring Signals

Current posting count and mix, the 3-5 most telling roles, repeated technology mentions with rough counts, and initiative language quoted briefly. If posting coverage looked partial (one ATS, one band of roles), say the view is incomplete.

## 8. Financial Indicators

Public: latest reported revenue/margin direction with filing citation. Private: funding history, investor names, and any credible revenue reporting, all labeled. If nothing credible exists, one honest sentence beats three speculative ones.

## 9. Strategic Initiatives and Momentum

What they appear to be investing in right now: product launches, partnerships, geographic expansion, AI adoption posture. Tie each to its evidence (press release, posting, exec interview).

## 10. SWOT

Four short lists. Every entry carries a citation or a confidence label. Strengths and weaknesses should be specific to this company, not category boilerplate ("strong brand" needs evidence).

## 11. Hypotheses Worth Testing

2-4 non-obvious, falsifiable readings of the evidence. Format each as:
- **Hypothesis:** one sentence. `(Hypothesis)`
- **Evidence:** the signals that suggest it, cited.
- **What to validate:** the question or data point that would confirm or kill it.

## 12. Discovery Questions

5-10 questions an account or consulting team should ask in a first meeting, each grounded in a specific gap or tension found above. Generic questions ("what are your priorities?") do not belong here.

## 13. Sources

Numbered list of every URL used, one per line. Mark any source that was fetched but only partially readable.

## 14. Coverage Notes

Three to six sentences of honest scope: pages that were blocked or missing, signal that was thin, anything time-sensitive (data gathered on DATE), and what the full primr pipeline would add for this company (deeper scrape coverage, more sources, cross-validation, QA-gated DOCX deliverable).
